Design notes for PM review — v4

  • New in v4 — Egyptian Blue brand-colour update (2026-08-29): the owner confirmed Egyptian Blue (#1034A6) as the brand's primary blue, replacing the deep-navy #0E1729 that previously did double duty as both the brand colour and the app's dark-surface colour. Nav/IA/layout from v2–v3 are untouched — this is a token-level colour pass. Because Egyptian Blue is a vivid, mid-tone colour (36% lightness) and not dark enough to hold contrast as a background fill, it was not a find-and-replace: every old-navy usage was audited individually and split into two roles — Egyptian Blue (--brand-blue) for brand moments — logo/brand-lockup (desktop top nav wordmark), the hero gradient, active/selected states (bottom-nav active icon, active search tab, active category chip, "added to compare" chip), the Feed tab's FAB, the feed creator-avatar accent, and the "Featured" business tag — versus a genuinely dark, near-black ink (--ink, #0A0F1C, blue-tinted) for real dark surfaces/backgrounds where Egyptian Blue would lose legibility — the "Featured businesses" dark showroom band, the stats band, the home footer band, the feed's base background, the floating compare-tray and toast pills, and the dark-outlined secondary pill button. Plain body/heading text on light surfaces (search input text, card titles) also now resolves to the new ink value rather than old navy, since that role was always "near-black text," not a brand-colour moment. Amber accents sitting on top of Egyptian Blue (the FAB's play icon, the "Featured" tag's dot) were contrast-checked — Egyptian Blue is lighter/more saturated than the old navy, so amber-on-brand-blue contrast is ~6:1 versus the old ~10.5:1; still comfortably above the ~4.5:1 floor for small UI accents, but noticeably closer to the line, so flag if it ever needs a bolder amber or a drop shadow behind it. White/ice text and icons on Egyptian Blue (chips, tags, tab pills) measure ~10:1 — no concern there. Verified in both the light and dark app-theme and at both breakpoints. The six thumbnail "out-of-focus footage" gradients (.grad-0….grad-5) were deliberately left alone — those are decorative video-still art direction, not brand chrome, and weren't part of this audit.
  • New in v3 — real desktop breakpoint (≥1024px): resize this window (or the browser) past ~1024px wide. Below that, this is still the exact v2 phone-in-bezel build, untouched. At and above it, the bezel/notch/status bar/bottom tab bar disappear and a persistent top nav takes over — per the brief's architecture section, "Web application" and "Mobile-first responsive interface" are related but distinct surfaces, and this is the first pass at the former. Every screen was reflowed for the extra width, not just centred/stretched: Home gets a wider hero and the category rail, trending-videos rail and featured-businesses rail become real multi-column grids instead of horizontal-scroll-only, alongside a 6-column "Explore categories" grid. Search gains a persistent filter sidebar (same filter state/markup as the mobile filter sheet, just always visible) beside a multi-column results grid instead of one stacked list. Video feed becomes a centred, narrower vertical column — closer to YouTube Shorts/TikTok's desktop web treatment than a stretched phone screen — with a live "Up next" rail alongside it that highlights the currently-playing Short as you scroll and jumps the feed on click. Business profile and product detail both go two-column: profile splits into a sticky info sidebar (identity/stats/contact actions) beside a wider Products/Videos/About tab column; product splits into a sticky gallery beside specs/price/supplier/related-products. Bottom sheets (filter/account/compare) become centred modal panels at this width instead of bottom sheets, since a sheet sliding up from the bottom of a browser window reads as a mobile pattern. Compare tray, sponsored/featured labelling, verification badges, light/dark toggle and all interaction logic are unchanged — this is a layout pass, not a new visual direction.
  • Status: Navigation, IA and interaction model are approved and unchanged in this pass — every screen, transition and gesture from v1 is intact. This revision is a visual-execution pass only, responding to "not premium enough" + "doesn't feel video-first enough."
  • Thumbnails, before → after: every video/product tile was a flat two-stop gradient + one centered emoji — the single biggest reason it read as a wireframe. Now each tile layers a multi-stop angled gradient with two off-axis "bokeh" glows, a film-grain texture (SVG turbulence, blended at low opacity), and a bottom vignette — closer to an out-of-focus video still than a colour chip. Icons remain as placeholders for real thumbnails/photos, unchanged in role.
  • Video-specific cues added: duration badges on every video tile (home rail, search results, profile videos tab); a looping scrub-bar under the feed's progress dots so the current Short visibly "plays"; a pulsing ring on the creator avatar for the in-view feed slide; a small animated equalizer next to "Shorts" in the feed topbar. None of this is decorative-only — it's the vocabulary that signals "this is a real video" at a glance.
  • Depth & glass, before → after: chrome that used to sit on flat opaque backgrounds — bottom nav, search header, filter tabs, profile tabs, product top bar, sheets — now uses frosted blur + translucency, echoing the feed rail's existing glass treatment so the whole app feels like one material system. Cards moved from a single flat shadow to a two-layer ambient+key shadow (see --shadow / --shadow-lg) that intensifies on hover/press instead of just shifting position.
  • Typographic contrast: hero headline, section titles, stat numbers and product price all stepped up in size and tightened in tracking (negative letter-spacing on display type) for a punchier display/body split — the brief for a video-first consumer app reads bigger and bolder than a B2B density-first one.
  • Bolder, more intentional colour placement: the "Featured businesses / Digital showrooms" row is now a dark navy showroom band (grain + teal glow, frosted-glass business cards) instead of a pale-grey tint — restores the brief's alternating dark/light rhythm, which the flat build had lost. Amber CTAs got a gloss gradient + glow instead of a flat fill; the compare tray, FAB and stat numbers pick up matching amber glow so paid/primary actions feel more alive without diluting amber's meaning (still reserved for CTA + Sponsored/Featured).
  • Small bug fix along the way: the home search button, the search-screen's back/search/filter icons were rendering as blank circles (icons were never filled in on load) — filled in, no behaviour change.
  • Sponsored stays legible and distinct: unchanged semantics — amber "Sponsored"/"Featured" tags, separate labelled row in search — now with a left accent bar and soft glow on sponsored result cards so the paid placement reads as a considered treatment, not just a tinted background.
  • Verification states: unchanged — teal check = verified, grey clock = pending. Search "construction" or "hardware" to see the pending state (BuildRight Hardware).
  • Compare remains the persistent cross-screen tray (up to 3 products) — untouched per the approved interaction model.
  • Account tab and location picker remain stubbed with a toast — onboarding/auth is a separate flow per the brief.
  • Toggle light/dark with the moon/sun icon in the hero, then re-run the same walkthrough: search "water tank suppliers near Nairobi", scroll the video feed, open AquaTank Kenya's profile, open a product, add 2–3 to compare.
